A loved front-end on a broken loop

Testers fall for the AI search and the taste level — then hit a wall the moment they try to transact. Nobody replies to enquiries, every rating is 5.0, and vendors are asked to pay to read a lead they can't verify. It demos brilliantly and converts poorly.

50 synthetic users across 10 segments (mobile buyers, older/less-techy, corporate, high-stakes, returning, prospective & active vendors, skeptics, accessibility, off-vertical). One cohort dropped to a transient error → 45 usable. All counts are out of 45.

What they loved

  • 15+  Natural-language search "just got it" — plain, even broken English → a relevant shortlist. Cited by grandmothers, ESL users and event pros alike.
  • ~12  The "Patch recommends" reasoning note — why the top pick fits the occasion/headcount/budget. Called "the actual magic."
  • ~11  Honest out-of-scope handling — "we don't cover that yet" instead of hallucinating results won trust even from people it turned away.
  • ~8  No commission / free to list / flat £29 — the model is understood and respected vs Bark/Checkatrade.
  • ~7  Vendor onboarding — the listing-strength meter, draft-then-publish, "preview as buyer." "It nagged me in a good way."
  • ~6  Save-heart shortlist + enquire to several at once. "Normally my whole afternoon gone."
  • ~5  No account needed to browse or enquire.
  • 3  Accessibility fundamentals — focus-trapping/restore in dialogs, working skip link, 250% reflow, reduced-motion. Rare praise from expert AT users.

Where segments diverge

Techy vs. not — the sharpest divide, on login. Passwordless is a delight for high-comfort users and a hard blocker for the over-60s and ESL, to whom "no password" reads as a scam signal. Same feature, opposite outcomes.

Mobile vs. desktop — the speed penalty lands on mobile. The 6–9s wait is a minor annoyance on desktop but an "it crashed" abandonment on phones and poor signal.

Buyer vs. vendor — same root, two faces. Buyers hit silence after enquiring; vendors hit pay-to-read-the-lead. Both are the same broken handshake — enquiries landing on unclaimed seed listings with no reachable human. Fix one side and the other's complaint disappears.

Stakes collapse trust tolerance. Casual users forgive the 5.0 stars and stock photos; procurement, finance, planners and parents treat the same signals as disqualifying — they won't stake a real booking on "a beautiful demo."

Highest-leverage fixes — frequency × severity

1

dataconfigClose the enquiry loop — stop enquiries hitting a void

The root of both buyers' and vendors' worst complaints. Get real, reachable vendors on the other end (claim seed listings / route to verified contacts), and stop promising a reply until that's true.

2

codedataKill the uniform 5.0 stars

Most-mentioned issue (24). Show "New / not yet reviewed" instead of a fake perfect score — an absent rating reads as more trustworthy than an unearned 5.0.

3

dataReal vendor photography

Directly undermines the "photo-led" positioning (15); pairs with #2 as the twin "this is just seed data" tell.

4

codecopySet honest expectations; flag unclaimed listings

Warn buyers when a listing is unclaimed/may not reply and drop the over-promise. Near-zero cost, big trust return.

5

configFix the two hard blockers: Stripe billing + email deliverability

A vendor literally couldn't pay; confirmation and alert emails aren't arriving. Low count, each a total dead-end on a critical path.

6

codeProgressive search status ("parsing… matching… writing notes")

Turns a 6–9s "it crashed" moment into visible work — cheap, saves the mobile abandoners.

7

codeconfigRescue low-tech login

A real 6-digit code path (needs the email template), reassuring copy on why magic links are safe, same-device handling. The difference between winning and losing the entire older/ESL segment — high-intent, real-event bookers.

8

copySignpost scope on the homepage — "Food & drink, London"

Eliminates the food-only + London-only wasted-time complaints in one line. The honest in-search message exists — just move the signal earlier.

9

codeproductReal hard filters — dietary (incl. nut-free), date/availability

Six requests, one safety-critical. Buyers want to lock constraints, not trust the vector match. (Note: this partly reverses the recent dietary-filter removal — worth a deliberate call.)

10

productRethink the vendor free→paid on-ramp

Pay-£29-to-read-an-unverified-lead + 1-photo free + paywalled analytics means the free tier can't prove its own value. Consider: first lead unlocked free, a visible "enquiries this month" signal, 2–3 photos on free.

+

codeAccessibility quick wins (one sprint)

Label the search input, announce the result count as a live region, add Escape-close + focus-trap to the mobile menu — cheap, and they matter to the procurement/public-sector evaluators.
